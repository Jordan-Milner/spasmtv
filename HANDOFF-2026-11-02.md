## The one gap that matters

**Our chat puts an emote image on about 1 line in 2.4. A real small room puts one
on about 1 in 23.** `% emote-only` matches (2.6 vs 2.8), so it is not emote spam —
it is an emote stapled to nearly every *text* line. Two causes, both real: the
prompt hands the model the channel's codes and law 1 tells it emotes are content
words, so it writes them into ~25% of lines; and the loaded 7TV/BTTV sets contain
single-token codes (`w`, `f`, `lol`) that the render regex matches against plain
text. The template layer's `kind === 'emote'` share (~18%) is a third.

I deliberately did **not** tune this. It needs a browser run to measure, and
tuning it blind is exactly how the bands went wrong in the first place. Target:
≤10% on the rubric, realistically ~5%.

## Retracted — do not re-derive these

Three claims made earlier in this session were wrong, and two of them were load-
bearing. All three are recorded so nobody spends a day rediscovering it:

1. **"The ≤10% emote band is a fabrication the corpus disproves" — false.** Law 1
   is right. My first parser had the `emotes` format backwards (`ranges/id`
   instead of `id:ranges`, colon-separated, UTF-32 code points) and silently fell
   back to guessing. **No band changes are needed** — the sim is what is out of
   range, not the rubric.
2. **"Pin the style examples, ~2.5× less prefill" — no.** transformers.js does no
   cross-call prefix caching, so pinning buys nothing. Not done.
3. **"The north star's seven invariants / How-to-check section"** — the file is 34
   lines, five invariants, no such section. Edit against the real text.

The general failure: some claims were built on text I had *reconstructed* from
context rather than re-read. Anything in this note came from a fresh read or a
fresh run. Re-verify before repeating.

## Still open, ranked

1. **Cut the emote saturation.** The only large authenticity gap left, and the
   cheapest experiment in the repo: stop offering codes on non-emote kinds (or
   instruct restraint), then re-run the harness. Measure, don't guess.
2. **Run the harness with `?minutes=10`.** `sceneLinkage` is n/a below 10 judged
   lines and informational below 25; 4-minute runs clear neither bar. The metric
   is dead weight until runs are long enough.
3. **Thread one `rng()` (mulberry32) through + accept `?seed=`** so the gate stops
   failing on a coin flip — `% ALL-CAPS` swings 9.6–19.5% run to run at n≈66, and
   `repeat: { min: 0.02 }` needs a ≥5× repeat a 66-line log cannot produce. Both
   are already marked `informational` for n<150; a seed makes the whole thing
   reproducible.
4. **`unsafeLine` misses leet profanity** — `n1gg3r`, `sh1t`, `k y s` pass today.
   Jordan explicitly dropped this; it stays dropped. It is the only hole I know of
   in the "a real person is on camera" invariant.
5. **`priority` is honest now but untested** — the queue is logic-verified only. No
   browser run happened this session.
6. **Corpus breadth.** One time-of-day, English, 6 rooms, no raids. A
   raid-inclusive round at a different hour, 2–3 rooms doing 5–15 lines/min, would
   move the laws more than another week of band-tuning.
7. **Phone is the worst version** (256 M, no mic, no ASR) and the zero-backend
   invariant forbids fixing it in place. Opt-in BYO OpenAI-compatible endpoint is
   the only route; it is a product decision, not a task.
